When looking at the Antecedent Precipitation Index for the winters of 2023 and 2026, in January of both years, precipitation was occurring across the western United States. A series of Atmospheric River (AR) episodes over California, Oregon and Washington resulted in API maxima over those regions. In particular, over the Olympic Mountains and Cascade Range of Washington and the Sierra Nevada Range in northern California. Generally moist conditions continued in February of 2023, but in early February 2026, dry conditions temporarily developed over northern California, before some improvement is seen at the end of the month. While March 2023 remained moist, in March of 2026, precipitation stops falling across the southwestern United States, and by mid-March the API has fallen to zero across the eniter southwestern quarter of the United States. Phase-Space Plots are a way to reduce the dimensionality of a dataset that is being analyzed. In this particular study we have three independent sets of data. These are: 1) Precipitation recorded over the western United States, 2) 500 hPa geopotential height fields over the northeastern Pacific Ocean and most of North America, and 3) 300 hPa isotach fields over the northeastern Pacific Ocean and most of North America. Each of these datasets are a collection of two-dimensional arrays with an associated time stamp. This makes three, 3-dimensional datasets, or about a 27 dimension dataset. Data can be grouped in any number of ways, and Phase-Space plots will be one way to visualize the various categorizations.

First off, let's take a look at the first three months of 2023. In this phase-space plot I compare the 50 wettest and 50 driest 6-hour time periods during those three months to the 500 hPa Heights Self-Organized Map (SOM) nodes that produced those precipitation episodes. This represents a little more than one-quarter of the time periods in a 3-month time period. For each of the time periods, precipitation was simply added up across the western United States domain. The region of the domain where the precipitation is not being considered, but the individual maps could certainly be consulted if we were interested in rainfall over certain locations. The Precipitation SOM could be helpful in dividing observed precipitation by region, but that will be addressed at another time.

I also included the SOM that was displayed at the top of the page for the Jan-Feb-March 2023 time period. The number in the right hand table are the date in MDD format. Each of the data points is for 12z that day. Some of the datapoints on the right will match to points on the left table, but the left hand table also includes datapoints from 0z, 6z and 18z, depending on when the precipitation field was observed. The main thing to notice on the left hand table is the clustering of dry nodes and wet nodes. There are a few exceptions, but in general dry nodes are grouped with other dry nodes, and wet nodes are clustered with other wet nodes.
